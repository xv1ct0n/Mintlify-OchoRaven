CyberLearn Hub exists because finding good cybersecurity training is surprisingly hard. The internet is flooded with outdated bootcamp ads, conflicting Reddit threads, low-quality YouTube playlists, and paywalled content that doesn't always deliver on its promises. If you've ever spent more time researching *how* to learn than actually learning, you know the problem firsthand. This knowledge base does the curation work for you — every resource listed here has been evaluated for quality, relevance, and up-to-date content so you can make an informed decision before spending your time or money.

## What's Inside

CyberLearn Hub is organized into five major sections, each designed to answer a specific question you might have as a learner.

- **Free Training** — Platforms, courses, and content you can access at no cost, from beginner-friendly intros to advanced technical deep dives.
- **Paid Training** — Premium platforms and instructor-led courses worth the investment, with honest notes on what each one is best suited for.
- **Certifications** — A breakdown of the most recognized cybersecurity certifications by level, with study resources, cost estimates, and exam tips.
- **Practice Labs** — Hands-on environments including CTF platforms, intentionally vulnerable machines, and live hacking ranges.
- **Domains** — Specialization hubs covering Network Security, Web Application Security, Cloud Security, Penetration Testing, and more.

## Who This Is For

CyberLearn Hub is built for three distinct audiences, and every resource listing notes which group it's best suited for.

**Beginners starting fresh** — You may be coming from a non-technical background, or you have some basic IT familiarity but have never studied security specifically. You need a clear on-ramp: foundational networking, operating system basics, and introductory security concepts before anything else.

**IT professionals pivoting to security** — You already understand infrastructure, systems administration, or software development, and you want to redirect that knowledge toward a security career. You need resources that acknowledge your existing skills and help you fill the specific gaps that matter for security roles.

**Experienced practitioners seeking advancement** — You're already working in security and want to go deeper into a specialization, pursue a high-value certification like OSCP or CISSP, or stay current with emerging threats and technologies. You need advanced content that doesn't waste time on basics you already know.
