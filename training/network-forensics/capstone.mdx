# Lab 7 — Network forensics capstone

This capstone removes most of the step-by-step guidance. Your goal is to investigate an authorized PCAP or Security Onion case using the workflow developed in Labs 1–6.

## Scenario

You receive a defensive lead involving a workstation that may have communicated with an unusual external destination. You have packet data and, where available, related Security Onion, Arkime, Suricata, or SIEM telemetry.

Your job is to determine what the evidence supports, what remains unknown, and what additional data would improve confidence.

## Required workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Define the investigative question">Write the question you are trying to answer before filtering packets.</Step>
  <Step title="Scope the evidence">Record capture start/end time, packet count, key hosts, protocols, and known gaps.</Step>
  <Step title="Triage endpoints and conversations">Use protocol hierarchy, endpoints, conversations, and focused filters.</Step>
  <Step title="Reconstruct relevant activity">Analyze DNS, TCP sessions, HTTP/TLS, and repeated communication as applicable.</Step>
  <Step title="Handle artifacts safely">Extract only approved lab objects, calculate hashes, and preserve provenance.</Step>
  <Step title="Correlate other telemetry">Use Security Onion, Arkime, Suricata, Elasticsearch/OpenSearch, or other available evidence without conflating their query languages.</Step>
  <Step title="Reproduce key findings">Use TShark to reproduce at least one important observation from the command line.</Step>
  <Step title="Write the report">Separate observations, interpretations, alternative explanations, and limitations.</Step>
</Steps>

## Minimum evidence requirements

Your report should include:

- capture scope;
- host(s) of interest;
- at least one DNS observation when DNS is present;
- at least one reconstructed TCP/application conversation;
- analysis of any repeated communication pattern;
- relevant artifact or IOC records;
- at least one correlation with a second telemetry source when available;
- exact filters or commands used to support key findings.

## Evaluation rubric

| Area | Strong result |
| --- | --- |
| Scope | Capture boundaries and evidence gaps are explicit. |
| Packet analysis | Filters and stream analysis directly support claims. |
| Correlation | Other telemetry is used to add context, not replace packet evidence. |
| Reasoning | Observation and interpretation are clearly separated. |
| Reproducibility | Another analyst can rerun key filters/commands. |
| Reporting | Findings are concise, evidence-based, and appropriately qualified. |

## Optional advanced extensions

- Write a Suricata rule against a benign lab pattern observed in the PCAP.
- Build an ES|QL, KQL, or OpenSearch query that searches indexed telemetry for the same behavior.
- Use Python to calculate beacon intervals from TShark output.
- Create a YARA rule for a harmless exported training artifact.
- Map relevant behavior to MITRE ATT&CK while documenting why the mapping is contextual rather than proof of adversary intent.

<Info>
The goal is not to find something malicious at all costs. A high-quality investigation may conclude that the available evidence supports benign activity, is inconclusive, or requires additional telemetry.
</Info>
