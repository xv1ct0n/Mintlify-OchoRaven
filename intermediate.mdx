Intermediate certifications are designed for professionals with one to three years of experience who are ready to move beyond foundational concepts and specialize in a specific discipline. At this level, you're no longer proving you understand what a firewall is — you're demonstrating that you can perform penetration tests, analyze threat intelligence, operate in a SOC, or assess enterprise risk with genuine competence. Choosing the right intermediate cert means matching your credential to your career direction and the expectations of employers in your target role.

<Accordion title="CEH (Certified Ethical Hacker)">
  **What it is:** EC-Council's Certified Ethical Hacker is one of the most recognizable names in offensive security certifications. It covers penetration testing phases, attack techniques, tools, and methodologies across a broad range of topics including network hacking, social engineering, web application attacks, and cryptography.

  **Who it's for:** Professionals targeting roles where a recognized offensive security credential is listed as a requirement, particularly in organizations or government contexts where EC-Council's brand recognition still carries weight with HR departments.

  **Exam cost:** $1,199 (includes official courseware); third-party prep options are significantly cheaper

  **Format:** 125 multiple-choice questions, 4 hours

  **Passing score:** 70% (varies slightly by exam version)

  **Career value:** Widely recognized as an HR filter — particularly in enterprise and government job postings — though frequently criticized by practitioners for its multiple-choice format and heavy emphasis on memorization over demonstrated skill. Valuable for opening doors, less so for proving hands-on ability.

  **Best study resources:**
  - EC-Council official courseware (included with exam purchase)
  - Matt Walker's CEH All-in-One exam guide (book)
  - Boson practice exams for realistic question prep
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="CompTIA PenTest+">
  **What it is:** CompTIA's penetration testing certification that bridges the gap between theoretical security knowledge and applied offensive skills. It covers planning and scoping, information gathering, vulnerability scanning, exploitation, post-exploitation, and reporting — combining multiple-choice questions with performance-based items.

  **Who it's for:** Security professionals who have Security+ or equivalent experience and want a DoD 8570-compliant penetration testing credential that demonstrates both technical knowledge and professional methodology.

  **Exam cost:** $370 (USD)

  **Format:** Up to 85 questions (multiple choice and performance-based), 165 minutes

  **Passing score:** 750 out of 900

  **Career value:** Respected in government and compliance-heavy environments due to DoD 8570 alignment. Considered a solid theoretical and practical bridge cert — though practitioners often pair it with a more hands-on credential like PNPT or OSCP.

  **Best study resources:**
  - Jason Dion's CompTIA PenTest+ course on Udemy
  - CompTIA's official study guide
  - TryHackMe's Jr. Penetration Tester path for hands-on reinforcement
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="CompTIA CySA+ (Cybersecurity Analyst)">
  **What it is:** CompTIA's blue team certification focused on threat detection, threat intelligence, security operations, vulnerability management, and incident response. It validates the skills required to operate effectively as a SOC analyst, threat hunter, or security operations professional.

  **Who it's for:** Security professionals moving into or advancing within defensive security roles — SOC analysts, threat hunters, incident responders, and vulnerability management professionals who want a recognized credential aligned to their day-to-day work.

  **Exam cost:** $370 (USD)

  **Format:** Up to 85 questions (multiple choice and performance-based), 165 minutes

  **Passing score:** 750 out of 900

  **Career value:** Increasingly requested for SOC analyst and threat intelligence roles, particularly in organizations that use CompTIA's framework for team certification requirements.

  **Best study resources:**
  - Jason Dion's CySA+ course on Udemy
  - Mike Chapple's CySA+ study guide (book)
  - Blue Team Labs Online and TryHackMe's SOC Level 1 path for hands-on practice
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="PNPT (Practical Network Penetration Tester)">
  **What it is:** TCM Security's fully practical penetration testing certification — and one of the most respected mid-level offensive security credentials available. The exam consists of a 5-day penetration test against a realistic network environment, followed by submission of a professional-grade written report. There are no multiple-choice questions.

  **Who it's for:** Aspiring penetration testers and red teamers who want to demonstrate real-world offensive skills rather than exam memorization. The PNPT is particularly valued by hiring managers who want to see that a candidate can actually perform a pentest and communicate findings professionally.

  **Exam cost:** $399 (includes two attempts)

  **Format:** 5-day practical penetration testing exam + professional written report, reviewed by TCM Security staff

  **Passing requirement:** Pass the practical exam and submit an acceptable penetration testing report

  **Career value:** Rapidly growing in recognition among security practitioners and hiring managers. Increasingly cited as a preferred alternative to CEH for roles requiring demonstrated offensive skill.

  **Best study resources:**
  - TCM Security Academy's Practical Ethical Hacking course (the primary prep course)
  - TCM Security's OSINT, Linux Privilege Escalation, and Windows Privilege Escalation courses
  - Practice environments on Hack The Box and TryHackMe
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="eCPPT (eLearnSecurity Certified Professional Penetration Tester)">
  **What it is:** INE's intermediate-to-advanced penetration testing certification featuring a fully practical exam format. The eCPPT covers web application penetration testing, network pentesting, privilege escalation, and report writing — all assessed through hands-on lab performance.

  **Who it's for:** Security professionals who have completed the eJPT or equivalent experience and want a practical mid-level offensive credential before attempting OSCP. It serves as an excellent stepping stone in the INE/OffSec learning progression.

  **Exam cost:** ~$200 standalone; also included with an INE Professional subscription (~$299/year)

  **Format:** Fully hands-on practical exam with report submission

  **Career value:** Solid mid-level recognition, particularly among practitioners familiar with the INE ecosystem. Less universally recognized than OSCP but valuable as a progression milestone.

  **Best study resources:**
  - INE's Penetration Testing Professional (PTP) course
  - TCM Security's course catalog for supplemental offensive skills
  - VulnHub and Hack The Box machines for additional practice
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="GIAC GSEC (Security Essentials)">
  **What it is:** The GIAC Security Essentials certification, developed by SANS Institute, covers a broad range of security topics including cryptography, access controls, network protocols, incident handling, and Linux/Windows security. It is one of the most respected broad-based security certifications in enterprise and government environments.

  **Who it's for:** Security professionals with a year or more of experience who want a rigorous, well-regarded credential recognized by enterprise employers, government agencies, and defense contractors. The GSEC validates breadth of knowledge rather than specialization.

  **Exam cost:** $949 (USD); typically taken after completing a SANS course, which adds significantly to total cost

  **Format:** 106–180 questions, 4–5 hours (open book — you may use your notes and index)

  **Passing score:** 73%

  **Career value:** Highly regarded by enterprise and government employers. The GIAC open-book format rewards thorough preparation and practical understanding over rote memorization.

  **Best study resources:**
  - SANS SEC401 (Security Essentials) course is the official preparation track
  - GIAC practice exams
  - Community-built GSEC indexes for the open-book exam format
</Accordion>

<Note>
  The PNPT is increasingly preferred over the CEH for practical penetration testing roles. While the CEH has stronger name recognition with non-technical HR departments, practitioners and hiring managers who understand offensive security consistently value the PNPT's real-world exam format. If you have to choose one, the PNPT better demonstrates actual pentesting ability — and costs less.
</Note>
